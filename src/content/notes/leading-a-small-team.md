I've led small teams twice: three engineers across five client projects at Nortik, and a frontend team of four at Florbs. Both times I was also writing code. What works for me:

## Make ownership explicit

Every feature has one owner from the first ticket to production. The owner doesn't do all the work, but they know its state and they decide.

## Reviews are for the team, not the gate

Code review is where conventions spread and where people learn the parts of the codebase they don't usually touch. I try to review quickly, explain the "why", and mark nitpicks as optional.

## Protect focus

Fewer meetings, written decisions, and one place to look for what matters this week.

## Stay hands-on, carefully

Writing code keeps my estimates honest and my reviews grounded. The trap is taking the critical-path work myself and becoming the bottleneck, so I pick the work that unblocks others instead.

## Say what good looks like

Clear expectations up front save hard conversations later.
